Prepare for a direct conversation with David about your house

A practical first-call worksheet for homeowners in Puget Sound and northwest Washington, starting with Snohomish and King counties.

Prepared by Washington Home Solutions LLC · Reviewed September 5, 2026

Describe the decision you are trying to make

Start with the reason a sale is on your mind and what you need to learn before deciding. Perhaps you are comparing a direct offer with listing, managing a property from elsewhere, or deciding whether to repair first. Tell David what would make a proposal workable. An inquiry is a starting point; it does not commit you to selling.

Separate property facts from estimates

Provide the address, your relationship to the owner, occupancy, and the condition you have actually seen. List unresolved questions separately: an old repair someone mentioned, a roof whose age is unknown, or a document you have not located. Photos of accessible spaces can help explain condition. Avoid sending account numbers or sensitive personal documents through an initial public inquiry form.

Explain access and timing honestly

Describe who can authorize a visit and which dates are practical. If someone lives in the property, explain that before arranging access. An inherited property, multiple owners, or an existing agreement can affect who needs to participate. Ask the appropriate professional to resolve authority questions; a buyer conversation cannot settle them.

Ask David to explain the offer assumptions

Ask which condition and repair assumptions affect a proposed price, what still needs to be reviewed, who would be named as buyer, and which closing conditions apply. Request the proposal in writing. Compare the actual proposed terms with the outcome you need, including possession, contents left behind, costs, and any remaining approvals.

Keep a short decision record

After the conversation, record what was confirmed, what remains open, who will follow up, and whether you want to continue. If a direct purchase does not fit, you can consider other paths independently. Use this worksheet to make the discussion concrete rather than rushing toward an unsupported price or date.
